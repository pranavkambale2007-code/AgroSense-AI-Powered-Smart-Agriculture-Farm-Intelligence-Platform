Agricultural & AI Disclaimer

Last Updated: October 23, 2026 • Mandatory Field Guidance

1. Probabilistic Nature of Artificial Intelligence

The machine learning models deployed within AgroSense are statistical classifiers trained on visual and agronomic datasets. Artificial intelligence outputs are inherently probabilistic estimates, not deterministic guarantees.

A high confidence score (e.g. 94%) indicates visual pattern similarity with historical training images; it is not definitive laboratory confirmation of a biological pathogen. Algorithms can produce inaccurate, incomplete, or unexpected classifications.

2. Plant Pathology & Disease Screening Limitations

The Plant Disease Screening module (#/disease) is designed as a frontline visual triaging aid:

  • Not a Laboratory Assay: Visual screening cannot differentiate between look-alike bacterial, viral, or abiotic physiological disorders with the fidelity of DNA/PCR sequencing or culturing.
  • Environmental Variability: Camera sensor quality, lighting glare, leaf wetness, nutrient scorch, and dust can degrade diagnostic accuracy.
  • Treatment Responsibility: AgroSense does not prescribe binding chemical interventions. Always check statutory state pesticide guidelines, active ingredient concentration limits, and withholding periods before spray applications.

3. Crop Recommendation & Soil Chemistry Coverage

Crop suitability recommendations are derived from multivariate matching of primary macronutrients (Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium), soil pH, and typical regional rainfall against ICAR reference guidelines:

  • Recommendations assume typical soil depth, adequate drainage, and normal tilth.
  • Local subsoil salinity, hardpan layers, root-knot nematode infestations, and unseasonal rainfall anomalies will alter actual agronomic outcomes.
  • Market demand, local storage infrastructure, and seed availability must be independently verified prior to planting.

4. Third-Party Meteorological & Satellite Data

Weather forecasts are fetched in real time from the Open-Meteo API. Satellite metrics and market price indices (e-NAM) reflect published external feeds. Third-party data may be delayed, intermittent, estimated, or revised retroactively. AgroSense is not liable for operational disruptions resulting from external data inaccuracies.

5. Measured Data vs. Mathematical Estimates

Users must distinguish between measured parameters and computational estimations:

  • Measured Values: Numerical figures obtained from certified soil testing laboratory reports or calibrated on-farm hardware sensors.
  • Estimated Values: Figures labeled as “simulated”, “modeled”, “Penman-Monteith ET0 estimate”, or “yield index projection”, which are calculated via empirical formulas.

6. Independent On-Ground Verification Required

Users are strictly advised to corroborate all machine-generated guidance with certified local agronomists, university extension specialists, or officers at your nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) before:

  • Purchasing large quantities of commercial fertilizer, seed varieties, or agricultural inputs.
  • Applying systemic fungicides, synthetic insecticides, or chemical herbicides.
  • Altering irrigation borewell investments or making major farm capital commitments.

7. No Warranty of Agricultural or Financial Outcomes

AgroSense makes no representations or warranties regarding actual crop yield volumes, financial revenue, market price realization, or environmental outcomes. Agricultural production is subject to weather extremes, biological pest vectors, market volatility, and biological contingencies that no computer software can predict with certainty.
